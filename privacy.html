Privacy Policy

Last updated: 7 October 2026

WonderWorld collects no personal information from children. The game runs entirely in your browser and your child's progress is stored only on your own device — we never receive it. The one exception is an optional mailing-list form in the Grown-Ups area, behind an adult check, which a parent may choose to fill in. Nothing else is ever sent to us.

What the game stores, and where

When your child plays, WonderWorld saves their progress using your browser's localStorage — a small amount of space on your own device, under a single key called wonderworld.save.v1. That save file contains the nickname your child chose, their character's appearance, their XP, level, gems, badges, and how far they've got in each world.

It also holds the learning record that makes the Grown-Ups dashboard work: how many questions were answered correctly and incorrectly in each subject, the difficulty level the math game has adapted to, a list of the last 60 activities completed, total time played, the number of sessions, and your sound and accessibility settings. This is the most detailed information in the game, and it is exactly the part that never leaves your browser.

All of that never leaves your device. We cannot see it. There is no account and no login, and your child's progress is never uploaded anywhere. We do run one small server endpoint, used solely for the grown-ups' mailing list described below; it never receives anything from the game. If you clear your browser data or use Private Browsing, the save is deleted and we have no copy.

You can erase everything at any time: open Grown-Ups → Reset all progress in the game.

Read-aloud and spoken praise

WonderWorld can read story passages aloud, and can say things like "Great job!" when your child wins. This uses a speech voice built into your own device. We send no audio anywhere and store no recordings.

Some browsers ship "cloud" voices that would send the sentence being spoken to the browser maker's servers. WonderWorld deliberately refuses those: it only ever uses a voice your device can produce offline, so the text being spoken — which occasionally includes your child's chosen nickname — never leaves the device. If a device has no offline voice available, the game simply stays quiet instead. You can switch spoken praise off entirely in Settings → Cheering voice.

We deliberately do not use

  • Advertising of any kind
  • Third-party analytics, trackers or advertising SDKs
  • Cookies for tracking or advertising
  • Social media logins, sharing or embedded feeds
  • Chat, messaging or any way for players to contact each other
  • Location data, contacts, camera or photos
  • The microphone — unless a grown-up explicitly turns on “talking out loud” with WonderTutor. It is off until then, and it is push-to-talk even after. See Talking out loud with WonderTutor below.
  • Loot boxes, gambling mechanics or randomised paid rewards
  • In-game purchases of any kind

The one thing we do collect — from grown-ups only

In the Grown-Ups section there is an optional form where a parent or guardian can join our family beta mailing list. The whole Grown-Ups area sits behind an adult check — a two-digit multiplication that has to be typed in — so a young child cannot wander into it, and it re-locks every time you leave.

Children's privacy (COPPA, GDPR-K and friends)

WonderWorld is designed for children aged roughly 5 to 12. The game itself collects no personal information from children at all — there is nothing for us to disclose, share, or ask consent for. The only thing we ever learn that touches on a child is the number of children a parent optionally volunteers on the mailing-list form: never a name, never an age, and never anything connected to how they play. The form is behind an adult check for exactly this reason. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 13. If you believe a child has somehow submitted personal information to us, please contact us and we will delete it immediately.

WonderTutor

WonderTutor is an optional learning guide inside the game. Most of what it does runs entirely on your device — the questions it asks, the marking of answers, the tracking of which skills your child has mastered, and the choice of what to teach next are all ordinary calculations here, with nothing sent anywhere.

When WonderTutor writes an explanation or answers a question your child typed, it sends a small amount of context to an AI provider through our own server. That context is built from a fixed list of fields and contains only:

  • The grade level a grown-up set, and the tutoring language
  • Which skill is being taught, and roughly what level it is at
  • Small counts, such as how many of the last few answers were right
  • The names of the game worlds your child has played — never anything from their save
  • A few lines of the current lesson, so the tutor can follow the conversation
  • The question your child typed, if they asked one

It does not contain your child’s nickname, your email address, their avatar, their progress, their XP, gems or crystals, any device identifier, or anything about where you are. Before each request leaves the device we check it again for anything that looks personal, and block it if we find any.

If your child types something personal — their full name, an address, a phone number, their school — we detect it on the device and do not send it at all. WonderTutor answers locally and gently steers back to learning.

We do not keep a log of what your child asks. The conversation exists while the lesson is open and is discarded when it ends. The grown-ups dashboard shows educational summaries — which skills are coming along, which need practice — not a transcript of your child’s questions.

Talking out loud with WonderTutor

This is the only feature in WonderWorld that sends anything from your microphone off this device, and it is off until you turn it on. We have written this section bluntly on purpose, because it deserves a real decision rather than a tick-box.

If you enable it, your child can hold a button, speak to WonderTutor, and hear it answer. To make that work, their speech is sent to our AI provider to be understood and replied to in the moment. That is unavoidable — it is what talking to a computer means — and it is the trade-off you are agreeing to.

What we do to keep it as narrow as possible:

  • It is off by default. A grown-up has to pass the adult check and agree on a page that says all of this.
  • There is no open microphone. Your child holds a button to talk. The microphone is switched off between turns — genuinely off, not listening and ignoring.
  • It is always visible. A red indicator and a written status line show whenever the microphone is live.
  • We do not record it. We store no audio, keep no recordings, and write nothing to our servers. The audio travels directly between your device and the provider; it does not pass through us.
  • We send nothing identifying with it — no nickname, no email, no account, no device identifier.
  • You can turn it off at any time in Grown-Ups → WonderTutor, and it stops immediately.
  • It is never required. Everything WonderTutor teaches works by typing and reading.

Our AI provider processes the speech in order to respond. We ask them not to retain it, but their handling of data in transit is governed by their terms, not ours. If you are not comfortable with that, leave this feature off — nothing else in WonderWorld is affected, and your child loses no learning.

WonderWorld+ subscriptions

WonderWorld+ is an optional subscription for grown-ups. It is not on sale yet — the screens exist in the app but no payment can be taken — and this section describes how it is built so that it is true from the first day it is.

  • The subscription state is stored on the device in its own key, wonderworld.entitlement.v1, completely separate from your child's save file. Nothing about a subscription is ever written into a child's progress, and nothing about a child's progress is ever read by the subscription code.
  • When it goes on sale, the payment is handled entirely by the app store. We never see or store a card number. Your store account is not linked to anything in the game.
  • A parent's email address, if you give us one on the mailing-list form, stays in the separate list described above. It is not joined up with a subscription, with a device, or with anything a child does.
  • Subscription controls sit behind an adult check, and nothing in the child's game shows a price or asks for money.

The app also keeps a small in-memory list of product events — things like "the WonderWorld+ page was opened" — purely so the app can behave sensibly during a session. It never contains a child's nickname or anything they did in a learning activity, it is never written to disk, and it is not sent anywhere. If that ever changes, this page is updated before the change ships.

Hosting and server logs

The site is hosted on Cloudflare Pages. Like every web host, Cloudflare automatically processes standard request information (IP address, browser type, requested file) to deliver the page and protect against attacks. These are ordinary server logs, retained briefly by Cloudflare and not used by us to build any profile of a visitor. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Changes to this policy

If we ever add a feature that changes what we collect — for example, optional parent accounts that sync progress between devices — we will update this page and the date at the top before that feature goes live.
